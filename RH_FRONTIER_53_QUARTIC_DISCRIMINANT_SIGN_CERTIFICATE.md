# Frontier 53 — Certified sign of the quartic discriminant factor

Date: 2026-09-16

Let q=t^2 with 0<=q<=1 and define
\[
p(q)=8q^3-32q^2+6q-7.
\]
The normalized quartic factor has
\[
\operatorname{disc}_y F=-629856\,q\,p(q).
\]

A direct exact derivative analysis gives
\[
p'(q)=24q^2-64q+6.
\]
Its two real critical points are approximately 0.0996 and 2.5671; the first lies in [0,1]. Evaluating p at the endpoints and at the interior critical point shows p(q)<0 throughout [0,1]. Therefore
\[
\operatorname{disc}_y F>0\quad(0<q<=1).
\]
Thus F(y,t) has three distinct real y-roots for every 0<|t|<=1.

This is an exact finite-degree sign certification. It does not identify which sign interval contains the Riemann value y=z/u^2 and therefore does not close the quartic Hermite condition.
