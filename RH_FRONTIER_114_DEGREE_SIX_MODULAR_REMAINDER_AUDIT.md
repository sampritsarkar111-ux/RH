# RH Frontier 114 — Degree-Six Modular Remainder Audit

Date: 2026-09-16

## Purpose

Test the immediate proof gate suggested by Frontier 113: whether modular completion can turn the exact four-copy degree-six theta kernel into a positive Gram object without assuming RH.

## Logical standard

The calculation must prove an identity valid for every admissible parameter, not merely produce positive numerical samples. Moreover, positivity must imply the required hyperbolicity condition; an SOS identity for a coefficient polynomial alone is insufficient.

## Exact audit protocol

For the four-copy kernel `K_4`, separate:

1. zero-mode terms;
2. nonzero theta-lattice terms;
3. inversion-paired terms under `t -> 1/t`;
4. cross terms generated by the completion;
5. the degree-six discriminant/Hermite obstruction.

Then seek a decomposition

`D_6 = sum_a |F_a|^2 + R_6`,

where every coefficient in the quadratic form defining `R_6` is independently nonnegative and the identity is exact.

## Failure criterion

If any coefficient or principal minor of the completed remainder changes sign as the modular parameter varies, the proposed degree-six Gram closure is false. If the remainder stays positive numerically but no symbolic positivity proof is obtained, the route remains conjectural.

## Critical anti-circularity condition

No zero may be represented as `-t^2` with real `t` unless real-zero location has already been proved. The audit must operate directly on the theta kernel or completed xi function.

## Result

At this stage, the available exact ledger establishes the following: the ordinary positive-measure representation gives positive moments but does not force the cubic hyperbolicity gate; the direct four-copy symmetrized theta kernel is sign-indefinite; and modular symmetry alone does not provide a positive remainder. Consequently, the degree-six calculation does not yet close RH.

## Next proof-bearing move

Derive the full unsummed Poisson/Jacobi transform of the four-copy kernel and identify whether its transformed cross terms admit a canonical positive-definite pairing. If not, the exact signed residual should be retained as the new obstruction rather than replaced by an unjustified positivity claim.
