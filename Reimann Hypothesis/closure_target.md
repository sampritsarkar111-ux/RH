# Closure target after the strict-consecutive correction

The research program can be reduced to a much smaller, exact target than “all arbitrary Toeplitz minors”.

Let
`F(z)=xi(1/2+sqrt(z))=sum_{n>=0} a_n z^n`,
with `a_n>0`, and let `T=(a_{j-i})_{i,j>=0}`, `a_m=0` for `m<0`.
Define the consecutive/solid Toeplitz minors

`D_{r,k}=det(a_{k+j-i})_{i,j=0}^{r-1}`,  `r>=1, k>=0`.

## Strict-consecutive reduction

The earlier finite counterexample `(2,2,2,2,1)` showed only that **nonnegative** consecutive minors do not, by themselves, force total nonnegativity. That objection does **not** apply to the strict problem relevant here.

Katkova's Theorem D in *Multiple positivity and the Riemann zeta-function* states that if all minors of a Toeplitz block of orders `1,...,m` formed from consecutive rows and consecutive columns are positive, then all minors of those orders are positive. Passing to every `m`, strict positivity of all `D_{r,k}` implies strict total positivity of the whole one-sided Toeplitz matrix. Michalowski's 2026 xi-coefficient paper uses exactly this strict consecutive-minor criterion.

Thus the correct closure target is:

`D_{r,k}>0 for every r>=1 and k>=0.`

## Why this is actually equivalent to RH for the xi function

The standard Aissen-Schoenberg-Whitney-Edrei characterization gives

`RH <=> F is PF_infinity`,

because `F` is entire of order `1/2` with positive coefficients.

For this particular `F`, RH also implies **strict** PF_infinity: under RH all zeros of `F` are negative, and there are infinitely many of them. Hence its genus-zero factorization has infinitely many positive factors

`F(z)=a_0 product_{j>=1}(1+alpha_j z)`,
`alpha_j>0`, `sum alpha_j<infinity`.

Every finite Toeplitz minor is then a nonnegative polynomial/limit generated by these positive factors; because there are infinitely many positive factors, every minor not forced to vanish by the one-sided triangular structure has a positive contribution. In particular every `D_{r,k}` is strictly positive. Conversely, strict positivity of all `D_{r,k}` implies PF_infinity by the strict consecutive-minor theorem, hence RH.

Therefore the sharp equivalence is

`RH <=> D_{r,k}>0 for all r>=1, k>=0`.

This is the key architectural correction: we do **not** need to prove arbitrary non-consecutive minors separately, and we do not need to assume that nonnegative consecutive minors are sufficient.

## Exact recurrence

The consecutive minors satisfy the Desnanot-Jacobi identity

`D_{r,k} D_{r-2,k} = D_{r-1,k}^2 - D_{r-1,k-1} D_{r-1,k+1}`

(with the natural boundary convention). Thus any successful proof must establish a genuinely positive curvature inequality for the lower-order determinants, or another mechanism that proves the right-hand side is positive.

## Current unconditional frontier

Michalowski (18 July 2026) proves

`D_{r,k}>0` whenever `r>=2` and `k>=10^18 r^3`,

using a certified saddle-point analysis, q-Pascal dilation semigroup, Banach-algebra majorant, and inertia preservation. The author explicitly leaves the complementary wedge `k<10^18 r^3` open.

So the remaining problem is now sharply formulated as:

> **Prove `D_{r,k}>0` in the complementary wedge, uniformly for every order `r` and every `k<10^18 r^3`.**

The dangerous shortcuts remain forbidden:
- finite zero verification is not an infinite proof;
- a global factorization using critical-line ordinates cannot be assumed;
- finite numerical determinant checks are evidence, not a proof;
- Desnanot-Jacobi alone does not propagate tail positivity into the wedge.

## New research direction

The most promising next target is therefore a **non-circular lower bound for the consecutive-minor curvature** that is compatible with Desnanot-Jacobi and does not encode the zero locations. A successful bound should bridge the `k~r` regime to the already-certified `k>>r^3` tail.
