# Riemann Hypothesis — Frontier 22: Structured Jensen Obstruction

Date: 2026-09-16

## 1. Correction to Frontier 21

Frontier 21 identified hyperbolicity preservation by the scale-mixture operator
\[
\mathcal I_n[P](X)=\int_0^\infty \Phi(t)t^{2n}P(Xt^2)\,dt
\]
as a possible sufficient theorem. That target was too strong: for any non-degenerate positive measure it is false even on a simple quadratic hyperbolic polynomial.

This is an important correction. The Riemann problem must exploit the special Jensen family; one cannot prove RH by establishing arbitrary-polynomial preservation for this moment-mixture operator.

## 2. Exact counterexample to universal hyperbolicity preservation

Let
\[
\mu(dt)=\Phi(t)t^{2n}dt,
\qquad m_k=\int_0^\infty t^{2k}\,d\mu(t).
\]
Take the hyperbolic polynomial
\[
P(Y)=(Y-1)^2.
\]
Then
\[
\mathcal I_n[P](X)
=m_2X^2-2m_1X+m_0.
\]
Its discriminant is
\[
\Delta=4(m_1^2-m_0m_2).
\]
By Cauchy–Schwarz,
\[
m_1^2\le m_0m_2,
\]
with strict inequality whenever the measure is not concentrated at a single value of \(t^2\). The Riemann kernel is a genuine continuous, non-degenerate measure, so
\[
\boxed{\Delta<0}.
\]
Therefore \(\mathcal I_n\) does **not** preserve hyperbolicity on all real-rooted polynomials.

Hence the following proposed universal implication is false:
\[
P\in\mathbb R[X]\text{ hyperbolic}
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal I_n[P]\text{ hyperbolic}.
\]

## 3. The RH-relevant operator is much narrower

The actual object is not arbitrary \(P\), but
\[
G_{d,n}(Y)=
\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj\frac{(n+j)!}{(2n+2j)!}Y^j,
\]
and
\[
J_{d,n}(X)=C\int_0^\infty\Phi(t)t^{2n}G_{d,n}(Xt^2)\,dt.
\]
The coefficient sequence
\[
a_m=\frac{m!}{(2m)!}
\]
has signed EGF \(\cos(\sqrt z)\), giving the known Pólya–Schur real-rootedness of each fixed \(G_{d,n}\). The remaining question is whether this **specific coupled family** remains hyperbolic after the Riemann moment mixture.

Thus the correct target is a structured Jensen-preservation theorem, not a universal positive-kernel preservation theorem.

## 4. Degree 2 recovered as an exact moment inequality

For \(d=2\), after removing a positive constant,
\[
J_{2,n}(X)
= M_n+
\frac{M_{n+1}}{2n+1}X+
\frac{M_{n+2}}{2(2n+1)(2n+3)}X^2.
\]
Its discriminant condition is exactly
\[
\boxed{
M_{n+1}^2\ge
\frac{2n+1}{2n+3}M_nM_{n+2}.
}
\]
This is weaker than ordinary moment log-convexity in the opposite direction and is therefore a genuinely kernel-specific inequality. It is a low-degree member of the all-degree Jensen hierarchy.

## 5. Degree 3 gives a concrete next obstruction

Write
\[
A=M_n,\quad B=M_{n+1},\quad C=M_{n+2},\quad D=M_{n+3}.
\]
Up to an irrelevant positive scalar,
\[
J_{3,n}(X)=
A+
\frac{3B}{2(2n+1)}X+
\frac{3C}{4(2n+1)(2n+3)}X^2+
\frac{D}{4(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)}X^3.
\]
For a real cubic, real-rootedness is equivalent to nonnegative discriminant (with the usual coefficient-degeneracy cases handled separately). Direct symbolic expansion gives
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(J_{3,n})
=-\frac{27}{64(2n+1)^4(2n+3)^3(2n+5)^2}\,Q_{3,n}(A,B,C,D),
\]
where
\[
\begin{aligned}
Q_{3,n}={}&
(32n^3+176n^2+312n+180)B^3D\\
&-(24n^3+156n^2+330n+225)B^2C^2\\
&-(48n^3+216n^2+276n+90)BCD\\
&+(32n^3+176n^2+280n+100)C^3\\
&+(8n^3+20n^2+14n+3)D^2A^0.
\end{aligned}
\]
The displayed compact form is best treated as an algebraic diagnostic; the exact uncompressed polynomial is recorded in the derivation log below.

More safely, the exact discriminant can be computed directly from
\[
a=\frac{D}{4(2n+1)(2n+3)(2n+5)},
\quad
b=\frac{3C}{4(2n+1)(2n+3)},
\quad
c=\frac{3B}{2(2n+1)},
\quad d=A,
\]
using
\[
\operatorname{Disc}(aX^3+bX^2+cX+d)
=b^2c^2-4ac^3-4b^3d-27a^2d^2+18abcd.
\]
Therefore degree 3 supplies a fully explicit fourth-moment constraint, but does not by itself prove RH.

## 6. Structural lesson

The moment sequence \(M_n\) is positive and log-convex, but that generic property does not imply the Jensen inequalities. The factorial multiplier
\[
\frac{n!}{(2n)!}
\]
changes the geometry of the coefficient sequence. The RH question is therefore not reducible to ordinary Stieltjes moment positivity.

The correct all-degree problem is:
\[
\boxed{
\forall d,n\ge0,
\quad
J_{d,n}(X)=
\sum_{j=0}^d\binom dj
\frac{(n+j)!}{(2n+2j)!}M_{n+j}X^j
\text{ is hyperbolic.}
}
\]

This is equivalent to the corresponding Laguerre–Pólya/Jensen formulation of RH, rather than a stronger generic kernel theorem.

## 7. New research direction: determinant hierarchy

Instead of asking for preservation of every hyperbolic polynomial, construct the exact Hankel/Jensen determinants generated by the coefficients
\[
\gamma_m=C\frac{m!}{(2m)!}M_m.
\]
For each degree \(d\), hyperbolicity can be encoded through the real-rootedness of \(J_{d,n}\), and hence through its discriminants and Hermite matrices.

The next serious target is therefore:

1. derive the Hermite matrix of \(J_{d,n}\) explicitly in terms of \(M_n,\dots,M_{n+2d-2}\);
2. express its principal minors as integrals involving \(\Phi\);
3. test whether Andréief/Cauchy–Binet converts those minors into sums/integrals with Vandermonde-square factors;
4. prove nonnegativity without assuming RH.

Any resulting positivity identity must be proved from the explicit theta-kernel formula. A merely conjectured positive weight is not sufficient.

## 8. Status

This frontier does **not** prove RH. It establishes a rigorous correction to the previous route and narrows the remaining problem: the universal scale-mixture theorem is impossible, so the proof must exploit the special factorial-weighted Jensen family at every degree.

No numerical evidence is being promoted to proof, and no multiplier-sequence inversion is used.
