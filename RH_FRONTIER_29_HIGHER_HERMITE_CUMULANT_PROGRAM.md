# Frontier 29 — Higher-Hermite Cumulant Program

## Objective

Frontier 28 converted the degree-two Jensen condition into a variance bound under the tilted theta measure. The next step is to determine whether the entire Hermite PSD condition can be expressed through a hierarchy of inequalities for the same tilted measures.

Let
\[
d\nu_n(u)=\frac{u^{2n}\Phi(u)\,du}{M_n},\qquad
M_n=\int_0^\infty u^{2n}\Phi(u)\,du.
\]
Set
\[
Y=u^2.
\]
Then
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_n}[Y^k]=\frac{M_{n+k}}{M_n}.
\]
Thus every finite collection of consecutive moments \(M_n,\ldots,M_{n+r}\) is encoded by the moment sequence of \(Y\) under \(\nu_n\).

## 1. Jensen coefficients as tilted moments

Ignoring the common positive normalization and retaining the alternating Taylor sign explicitly,
\[
\gamma_{n+j}=C(-1)^{n+j}\frac{(n+j)!}{(2n+2j)!}M_{n+j}.
\]
Therefore the normalized Jensen coefficients are
\[
\boxed{
\frac{\binom dj\gamma_{n+j}}{\gamma_{n+d}}
=
(-1)^{j-d}\binom dj
\frac{(n+j)!}{(n+d)!}
\frac{(2n+2d)!}{(2n+2j)!}
\frac{M_{n+j}}{M_{n+d}}.
}
\]
Since
\[
\frac{M_{n+j}}{M_{n+d}}
=\frac{\mathbb E_{\nu_n}[Y^j]}{\mathbb E_{\nu_n}[Y^d]},
\]
all normalized Jensen coefficients are explicit rational transforms of a finite moment vector of \(Y\).

## 2. Exact higher-degree problem

Newton's identities turn these coefficients into power sums
\[
s_k=s_k\bigl(b_1,\ldots,b_{\min(k,d)}\bigr),
\]
and hence
\[
H(p_{d,n})=[s_{i+j}]_{0\le i,j<d}.
\]
Consequently, for each fixed \(d,n\), every Hermite principal minor is an explicit polynomial/rational expression in
\[
\mathbb E_{\nu_n}[Y],\ldots,\mathbb E_{\nu_n}[Y^d].
\]

The exact global theorem required is therefore a family of nonlinear moment inequalities satisfied by the particular tilted theta measures \(\nu_n\).

## 3. Why ordinary moment positivity is insufficient

The Hankel moment matrices of a positive measure are PSD. That fact controls expressions of the form
\[
\left[\mathbb E(Y^{i+j})\right].
\]
But the Hermite matrix here is built from Newton sums of normalized Jensen coefficients, not directly from the Hankel moment matrix. The map
\[
(M_n,\ldots,M_{n+d})\mapsto H(p_{d,n})
\]
is nonlinear.

Hence no implication
\[
\text{positive theta measure}\Longrightarrow H(p_{d,n})\succeq0
\]
may be asserted without a separate theorem.

## 4. Candidate non-circular closure theorem

A sufficiently strong theorem would be:

> For every \(d,n\), the Newton-Hermite matrix generated by the exact theta-weighted moments satisfies all principal-minor inequalities required for PSD.

Equivalently, one could seek an explicit root-independent kernel \(K_{d,n}(u,v)\) such that
\[
\boxed{
H(p_{d,n})=
\iint K_{d,n}(u,v)K_{d,n}(u,v)^T\,d\nu_n(u)d\nu_n(v),
}
\]
with the integrand interpreted as a PSD matrix-valued kernel. Such a formula would bypass the nonlinear obstruction only if coefficient identification is proved exactly.

## 5. Falsification gate

Before attempting to promote this program to a theorem, any proposed universal cumulant inequality must be tested against arbitrary positive measures. If it fails for generic measures, that is acceptable and informative: the missing ingredient must then be a special structural property of the Riemann theta kernel, not positivity alone.

A proposed identity is rejected immediately if it:

1. assumes the zeros of the Jensen polynomial are real;
2. defines the positive measure using those unknown zeros;
3. invokes RH or Laguerre–Pólya membership as an input;
4. proves only finitely many \((d,n)\);
5. replaces exact inequalities by numerical evidence.

## Status

This is a higher-degree research program built from exact algebraic reductions. It does not establish the required inequalities and therefore does not prove RH.
