# Riemann Hypothesis — Frontier 15: Attempted Finite-Strip Closure

Date: 2026-09-16

## Objective

Use the 2026 simultaneous hyperbolicity wedge to reduce RH to the remaining finite-strip region in the Jensen index plane.

Known unconditional input from the current literature has the form
\[
n^3\log^2(n+2)\ge Kd^5\implies J_{d,n}\text{ hyperbolic}
\]
for an absolute constant \(K>0\). Thus any possible non-hyperbolic Jensen polynomial must lie below that boundary.

## Attempted closure

A tempting argument is to say that the remaining region is finite. That is false: the complement of the wedge is an infinite region in \((d,n)\), even though it is constrained by an explicit growth inequality.

A second tempting argument is that for each fixed \(d\), only finitely many \(n\) remain. That is true, but it does not give a uniform bound over all degrees \(d\). The number and size of exceptional shifts may grow with \(d\).

A third tempting argument is to verify a large finite rectangle computationally. That still cannot cover infinitely many degrees.

## Required new theorem

To close this route one needs a uniform theorem controlling the complement of the wedge, for example an inequality of the form
\[
\text{all }d,n\ge0\implies J_{d,n}\text{ hyperbolic},
\]
or a bound reducing every exceptional case to an explicitly finite set with a proof that the reduction is uniform in \(d\).

No such uniform reduction has been established here.

## Status

The 2026 wedge materially sharpens the research target but does not itself prove RH. The finite-strip closure remains the unresolved step.
