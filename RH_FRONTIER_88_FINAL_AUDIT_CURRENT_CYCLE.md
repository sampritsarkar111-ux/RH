# Frontier 88 — Final audit of current RH cycle

Date: 2026-09-16

## Result

The current cycle produced exact reductions and several rigorous no-go results, but did not produce the missing unconditional all-degree theorem required for RH.

## New work in this cycle

- Frontier 83: adversarial audit of a 2026 Jensen–Pólya/bivariate-stability claimed proof; the continuous positive-integration step was not established by its cited source, and the generic principle is false.
- Frontier 84: exact obstruction to a global common-proper-position rescue; scaled root configurations cease to interlace when scales are sufficiently separated.
- Frontier 85: reduction of the FEATAM mixing step to a theta-specific diagonal coefficient multiplier and an all-order Hermite/Gram preservation target.
- Frontier 86: consolidated all surviving proof-bearing endgames and eliminated generic shortcuts.
- Frontier 87: explicit degree-two counterexample showing that positive diagonal coefficient multipliers do not generically preserve hyperbolicity.

## Remaining theorem

A complete proof still requires at least one unconditional result of the form
\[
\forall n,d\ge0,\qquad J_{d,n}\text{ is hyperbolic},
\]
or an equivalent theorem proving all required Toeplitz minors, self-weighted Hamburger matrices, or a de Bruijn–Newman endpoint inequality.

## Integrity condition

No numerical verification, finite-degree evidence, asymptotic wedge, unverified citation, or RH-equivalent positivity assumption is promoted to a proof.

## External status check

The Clay Mathematics Institute currently still lists the Riemann Hypothesis as an unsolved Millennium Prize Problem. Its current page notes that the first 10 trillion nontrivial zeros have been checked computationally, while an all-zero proof remains required.

## Bottom line

The repository is substantially more constrained and better audited than before, but RH is **not solved** by the current work. The next genuine breakthrough must supply the missing infinite, non-circular, theta-specific real-zero/positivity theorem.
